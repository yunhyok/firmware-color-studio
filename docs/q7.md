# PENTAX Q7 camera pack

The `pentax-q7` pack makes DCP (and DCP + XMP) profiles for the PENTAX Q7 from the colour tables and
calculations of its firmware 1.01. It is selected in the camera menu at the top of the window, with
`--camera pentax-q7` on the command line, or by opening a Q7 DNG. The working record of the reverse
engineering, with how each finding was obtained, is [research/q7-port.md](research/q7-port.md).

## What the pack reproduces

| Stage | Firmware routine | In Studio |
|---|---|---|
| Colour matrix: saturation (or monochrome filter) × Custom Image × sensor matrix, 1.0 = 256 | `ColorMatchingService_MakeColorMatrix` | `Q7Engine.color_matrix` |
| YC conversion with hue rotation, AdobeRGB variant, toning offsets | same routine | `Q7Engine.yc`, `chroma_offset` |
| Gamma table from key and contrast (4096 entries) | `GammaCorrectionService_CalcGammaCorrectionTable` | `Q7Engine.gamma` |
| Reversal Film gamma table | `GammaCorrectionService_CalcGammaCorrectionTableReversal` | `Q7Engine.gamma` |
| Basic gamma table of the hardware gamma block (4096 entries) | `GammaCorrectionService_CalcBasicGammaCorrectionTable` | `Q7Engine.basic_gamma` |
| 9 × 9 × 9 table: gamma(dark chroma suppression(matrix × RGB)) at each point | `ColorMatchingService_MakeColorReproductionLutParam` | `Q7Engine.reproduction_table` |
| Looking a colour up: basic gamma, then interpolation in the table | image processor hardware | `CameraColour._table_codes` |
| Highlight chroma suppression: whole up to luma 200 of 255, then a straight line to zero at white | image processor hardware; the level 200 is in `arcntChromaSuppression` | `CameraColour.chroma_gain` |
| Factory settings of each Custom Image | `cntCustomImageUnit<Mode>` records | `Q7Engine.factory_settings` |
| DNG colour matrices (illuminant A, D65) | `arcnlDNGColorMatrix1`, `2` | `Q7Engine.dng_color_matrices` |

Ten Custom Images are offered: Natural, Bright, Portrait, Landscape, Vibrant, Radiant, Muted, Bleach
Bypass, Reversal Film and Monochrome. Saturation, hue, key and contrast follow the firmware's rules:
Muted and Bleach Bypass have a toning colour in place of hue, Monochrome has key and contrast, and
Reversal Film has no settings. The Q7 has no highlight and shadow contrast settings. Cross Processing
uses per-channel curves and is not included; neither are the monochrome filter effect and monochrome
toning settings (the factory values are used).

## How the camera applies the tables

The Q7 does not apply matrix and gamma as separate stages. White-balanced sensor RGB goes through the
basic gamma table, and the result addresses a table of 9 × 9 × 9 colours that the firmware fills for the
Custom Image and its settings. The basic gamma table is the same for every Custom Image. The nine points
per axis are the inputs `0, 75, 179, 337, 591, 1060, 1845, 2876, 4095` (of 4095): the levels at which the
basic gamma reaches `0, 512, … 3584, 4095`. Studio builds both tables as the firmware does and
interpolates **tetrahedrally** between the points. The firmware does not say how the hardware
interpolates; the photograph below decides between the two common rules.

The gamma routines take a run-time ratio. Studio uses the word `0x2000`, which gives the tone gain
`v = 512`. The firmware writes this value itself where it prepares a second set of tables, the K-3 Mark III
has the constant 512 in the same place of the same calculation, and the photograph confirms it. With it the
reference grey (118 of 255) is at one eighth of the sensor's range.

## How it was checked

- **The integer calculations equal the firmware's.** `tools/q7/check_port.py` runs the five routines of
  the unpacked firmware in a CPU emulator and compares every output value with `q7_engine.py`: 276 colour
  settings (colour matrices, YC matrix, chroma offsets), 1,636 gamma tables of 4096 entries, and for 794
  settings the basic gamma table and all 729 points of the three-dimensional table are identical. This
  needs the firmware file and is not part of the automatic tests; `tests/test_q7.py` keeps values from
  that comparison.
- **The tables were extracted twice.** The unpacker in `tools/q7/` and the one in
  [pentax-q7-firmware-analysis](https://github.com/yunhyok/pentax-q7-firmware-analysis) were written
  independently; the unpacked segments have the same SHA-256 and the exported tables are byte-identical.
- **A Q7 photograph.** One DNG of a ColorChecker Classic (firmware 1.01, ISO 100, Landscape at its
  factory settings, sRGB) holds the camera's own JPEG of the shot at 1920 × 1280. Studio read the
  mosaic, built the Landscape profile and compared the 24 patches with that JPEG.
  [Measurements](chart-session-validation.json) · [script](../tools/chart_session.py)

  | Rendering of the DNG | Mean ΔE76, 24 patches | 18 colours | 6 greys | Largest |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | **Landscape DCP, the file's exposure** | **1.22** | 1.22 | 1.22 | 1.91 (orange) |
  | The same table interpolated trilinearly | 3.03 | 2.84 | 3.61 | 9.68 |
  | Matrix, clip and gamma without the table | 3.00 | 3.65 | 1.05 | 12.39 |
  | DCP of Bright / Portrait / Vibrant / Natural | 3.94 / 5.20 / 6.88 / 11.80 | | | |

  With trilinear interpolation the greys alone are off by 3.1 L\*; with the tetrahedral rule they are off
  by 0.9 L\*. The tone gain `v = 512` meets the six greys at +0.03 EV of the sensor level, the fallback
  `v = 256` only at −0.94 EV and with three times the grey error.
- **The DNG agrees with the firmware.** `ColorMatrix1` and `ColorMatrix2` of the file equal
  `arcnlDNGColorMatrix1` and `2`; the maker note's tag `0x021c` equals the firmware's sRGB to AdobeRGB
  matrix; the recorded saturation, contrast and sharpness equal the Landscape record; the maker note's
  model number 77690 is the number in the firmware's trailer.
- **The D65 DNG matrix equals a published one.** The firmware's `arcnlDNGColorMatrix2` is the matrix
  LibRaw lists for the Q-S1, which has the Q7's sensor.
- **The same engine family.** Factory settings and toning offsets read from the Q7 firmware equal those of
  the K-3 Mark III pack for the Custom Images both cameras have. The composition rates the basic gamma
  uses (`cnwHighlightShadowCompositionRate2048`) equal the K-3 Mark III's transition weights.

What these JPEGs changed in the pack (v0.4.0):

- **Highlight chroma suppression.** With the v0.3.0 model every colour mode missed the same patch, yellow, by ΔE 7 to 14: the camera keeps more blue in it. Over all the JPEGs the camera's chroma equals the model's up to luma 200 (of 255) and then falls: 0.83 of the model's at luma 210, 0.74 at 215, 0.57 at 225. The firmware hands the image processor a chroma suppression block whose parameters (`arcntChromaSuppression`, the same for every sensitivity) contain the level 200. Studio now scales chroma by `(255 − luma) / 55` above 200. The level is the firmware's; the straight line to zero at white is read from the JPEGs, because what the block's other parameters mean is not known.
- **Toning before the suppression.** With Bleach Bypass toned green the camera's white patch keeps 0.4 of the tint the mid greys have. The toning offset is part of the YC conversion and precedes the suppression; v0.3.0 added it afterwards.
- **Settings tags.** The Natural JPEG was developed with saturation −1, hue −1, key −1 and contrast +1 and its profile fits as well as the factory ones, which confirms the tag values for non-zero hue and key. Tag `0x007f` is 0 (off) in the Muted JPEG and 65535 in modes without toning: Muted's toning colour is in the same tag as Bleach Bypass's.
- **Reversal Film and white balance.** The camera developed Reversal Film with other white balance gains (red 1.594, blue 1.898) than the other ten (1.582, 1.949) from the same DNG. The comparison uses the gains recorded in each JPEG. The firmware explains it, without further photographs: with white balance on Auto, Reversal Film alone is calculated as the **Daylight** setting, and the Q7 derives a Daylight white balance from the parts of the scene it classes as daylight-lit when there are any, so the result is neither the automatic one nor the nominal Daylight preset (1.753, 1.868). This depends on the scene and is not something a profile can hold. With a preset or manual white balance chosen in the camera, Reversal Film changes nothing. [How this was traced](research/q7-port.md#reversal-white-balance) · check: `tools/q7/white_balance.py`

Cross Processing has no profile. The Monochrome JPEG has no colours to find the chart by; its patch positions are those of the JPEG stored in the DNG.

## Exposure anchor

`BaselineExposureOffset` is +0.5 EV, the K-3 Mark III pack's value. The Q7 writes `BaselineExposure`
−0.5 into its DNG, so the two cancel and the tone curve receives the sensor level, which is what the
camera's own processing does. In the photograph the profile meets the JPEG's greys best at +0.06 EV
from there.

The first comparison (v0.3.0) used the 1920 × 1280 JPEG stored in the DNG and a model without the highlight
chroma suppression: 1.49, with yellow off by 7.60. The table above is the v0.4.0 model on the same JPEG.

## RAW input

Studio opens native Q7 DNG files: 4064 × 3000, 12 bit, with the file's black and white levels, as-shot
white balance, default crop (the camera's aspect ratio setting) and orientation. Opening one selects the
Q7. **Use shot settings** takes the Custom Image, saturation, hue, key and contrast from the maker note,
and the toning colour of Muted and Bleach Bypass. The tag meanings are those of ExifTool's Pentax table;
the camera's developments confirm them, including non-zero hue and key. **Import body calibration from DNG** accepts a Q7 DNG when the
Q7 is selected.

## What is not verified

- **One scene.** One body, one ISO, one exposure in daylight, sRGB, developed ten ways. Settings other
  than those in the table rest on the emulator comparison.
- **The chroma suppression block.** Only its start level is taken from the firmware. The fall to zero at
  white fits the JPEGs (luma 200 to 225); what happens in deep shadows, where the block may also act,
  could not be seen in a chart whose darkest coloured patches are brighter than that.
- **Lightroom.** No Q7 DNG has been rendered with these profiles in Lightroom Classic. The comparison
  above uses Studio's own DCP renderer, which earlier releases compared with Lightroom on K-3 Mark III
  files ([validation](validation.md)).
- **Run-time gains.** The exposure gain and ratio words are taken at their nominal values. What the camera
  writes there at other sensitivities, or with highlight or shadow correction on, was not examined, and
  the basic gamma is ported for the nominal values only.
- **The hardware.** Tetrahedral interpolation and the position of the last table point are inferred from
  the photograph and from the table levels, not read from the image processor.
- **AdobeRGB.** The firmware changes only the YC matrix and the toning offsets for AdobeRGB; no routine
  that re-encodes the gamma table for AdobeRGB was found. The pack does the same.
- **Toning with the table.** DCP + XMP profiles place the table behind Adobe's clip of the matrix output.
  Colours the matrix does not push out of range are the same as in the plain profile; clipped ones are
  taken at the edge of the range.
- **Another camera's RAW.** With the Q7 selected and a K-3 Mark III RAW open (or the reverse), the preview
  passes the image through both sensor matrices; it shows the look, not that camera's exposure.
